## First 10 seconds

- **Clear goal in ~10 seconds, first reward within ~30.** New players should understand what to do almost instantly and get a payoff within 20–30 seconds. Keep the tutorial to the core loop only and hands-on, cut walks, waits and extra clicks (like pressing Play or walking to a lobby object afterwards), and simulate an impatient 7–8 year old to find the first three moments of boredom or confusion. ## Progression pacing

- **Show what's coming next.** Let players see future rewards, upgrades and milestones before they unlock them, so there's always a visible next target. — @momentumcash, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@momentumcash/video/7704836092024917279), 2026-11-05
- **Hand out small wins early and often.** Don't make players grind ~20 minutes before they feel they've achieved anything; front-load quick, cheap wins. — @momentumcash, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@momentumcash/video/7704836092024917279), 2026-11-05
- **Drip-feed systems.** You don't need to show every feature in the first five minutes. Introduce mechanics one at a time as the player is ready. ## Map and exploration

- **Use environmental cues and details to drive exploration.** Have an agent find empty, repetitive or confusing areas and spots a first-time player would overlook, then add visual cues, landmarks and small living details; borrow from Minecraft's around-the-corner curiosity, Elden Ring's clues instead of instructions, Rockstar's ambient details, supermarket layouts and interactive museum exhibits. ## Borrowed design psychology

- **Borrow proven loops from Pokemon, Animal Crossing and Duolingo.** Study how Pokemon makes collecting exciting, how Animal Crossing builds attachment through customisation, how Duolingo makes small wins feel rewarding, and how Rocket League adds skill expression without complex controls, then apply those to your collection, progression feedback and mastery curve. ## Tension

- **Build suspense by telegraphing danger and limiting information.** Use Hitchcock's bomb theory (players knowing a threat is coming) and FNAF-style limited information to create tension in your game's encounters. ## Idea generation

- **Mine dead, old and offline games for fresh mechanics.** Have an agent research shut-down multiplayer games with passionate communities (and what players still miss), 2005-2015 games, board games, real professions for job-sim tasks, and psychology experiments on cooperation and competition, then reinvent those mechanics as original Roblox concepts. ## Planning

- **Write a game design document before opening Studio.** Plan the whole game in a GDD first: onboarding/tutorial, core loop, map, player progression, art style and aesthetics. It stops you drifting between half-built systems and losing track of progress. ## Scope

- **Nail one replayable core loop before adding features.** Test the game with coins, XP, rewards and polish stripped out: if the bare loop isn't fun to replay, fix the mechanic instead of piling on rewards or features. Start small and expand from a working loop; switch to a smaller idea if your dream project is beyond your time and money. ## Game feel

- **Make the most common actions feel great: sound, animation, feedback.** List your three most frequent player actions and polish their animation timing, sound effects and visual feedback (Nintendo-style juice). Treat sound as important as visuals; many devs ship weak or missing SFX. ## Playtesting

- **Use blind playtesters, because you are biased toward your idea.** Just tell testers to join, with no explanation of how it works, then watch and listen to where they get stuck. Run several rounds (friends first, then a small Discord group of about 16), and you can thank testers with exclusive in-game items. ## Finding ideas

- **Port a simple real-life playground game; idea beats complexity.** Hits like Huss Valley (sharks and minnows / British bulldog with a meme name) and Don't Splash (an obby where touching water sends a monster after you) were simple, AI-built and fun to replay. A fun replayable idea matters more than complex maps, animations or systems. (x4) — @adamdoesroblox, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamdoesroblox/video/7693675980132453663), 2026-10-06; @adamdoesroblox, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamdoesroblox/video/7693711794354933023), 2026-10-07; @adamdoesroblox, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamdoesroblox/video/7695575216805661982), 2026-10-12; @adamdoesroblox, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamdoesroblox/video/7701834874398723359), 2026-10-28
- **Mine browser game portals and X for mechanics to adapt.** Browse sites like CoolMath Games and Poki, upcoming games posted on X, and Steam for fun mechanics that would work on Roblox. Adapt the mechanic with your own art and names rather than cloning someone's IP. — @adamdoesroblox, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamdoesroblox/video/7703284232587595038), 2026-11-01

- **Layer several simple reward systems on one easy core loop.** A hit fishing game (~25k CCU) is not polished, but its loop is simple and replayable, and it stacks rebirth, a skill tree and a rarity-based gambling-style drop system, each giving another small dopamine hit. Many cheap hooks on a clear loop can matter more than polish. Note the gambling-style mechanic is aimed at kids, so treat it carefully. — @dsrstudiorblx, [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@dsrstudiorblx/video/7707473569810320654), 2026-11-13
